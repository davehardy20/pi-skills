# MISSION.md Format

`MISSION.md` lives at the teaching workspace root. It captures why Dave is
learning this topic. Every lesson, resource choice, exercise, and next-step
recommendation should trace back to it.

## Template

```md
# Mission: {Topic}

## Why
{1-3 sentences. The concrete real-world goal Dave is chasing. What changes in
his work or life when he has this skill? Avoid abstract framings like "understand
X"; push for the outcome underneath.}

## Success looks like
- {A specific observable thing Dave will be able to do}
- {Another specific observable thing}
- {Another concrete signal of success}

## Constraints
- {Time, budget, prior commitments, learning preferences, tools, access, or any
  other constraint that bounds the approach}

## Out of scope
- {Adjacent topics Dave explicitly does not want to chase right now}
```

## Rules

- **One mission per workspace.** Unrelated topics need separate workspaces.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Ship a Rust CLI my team can use" beats "learn
  Rust." "Run a half marathon by October" beats "get fitter."
- **Push back on vagueness.** If the why is unclear, interview Dave before
  writing lessons.
- **Keep it short.** If `MISSION.md` runs past a screen, it has stopped being a
  compass and started being a plan.
- **Revise when reality shifts.** Confirm with Dave before changing the mission.
  If a learning-driven shift is accepted, update `MISSION.md` and add a learning
  record explaining the shift.
